Moving Étude to its own home

Its own domain, email address, Google account, GitHub space, Cloudflare account, server and social media names, all separate from your website. The names below are etudechess.com, hello@etudechess.com and the handle etudechess. About two hours of your time over a few sittings, and roughly US$11 a year for the domain; everything else is free.

What it will look like

  • The domain etudechess.com, bought through Cloudflare.
  • An email address on the domain, hello@etudechess.com. Cloudflare Email Routing forwards everything sent to it into your personal Gmail, so there is no new inbox to check. (Replies go out from your personal address unless you set up sending later; see “Later”.)
  • A Google account on that address (no Gmail inbox, just the login). Sites that offer “Sign in with Google” use it, so Étude's accounts hang off one login with one password, separate from yours.
  • A GitHub organisation, etudechess, holding the repo etude. Your own GitHub login owns it, so there's no second login on your PC. Its contact email is hello@etudechess.com.
  • A Cloudflare account holding the domain, the email routing and one Worker, etude, that serves both the app and the server on etudechess.com.
  • The handle etudechess on the social platforms, claimed now, used when you're ready.
  • A new, empty server. A Cloudflare account's stored data can't be moved to another account, so the new Worker starts with no accounts, friends or games. Your old Worker keeps running in your current account until everyone has moved.

The steps

1 · The Cloudflare account and the domain you

  1. Sign out of Cloudflare (or use a private window), then sign up at dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up with your personal Gmail plus +etude before the @, for example yourname+etude@gmail.com. Gmail delivers it to your normal inbox, and Cloudflare treats it as a separate address, so it's a separate account from the one your current server is in. Confirm the email Cloudflare sends.
  2. Turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app: profile → Authentication. Save the backup codes in your password manager.
  3. Domain Registration → Register Domains: search etudechess.com and press Purchase. Choose 1 year (auto-renew stays on), enter contact details and a card, accept the agreements, Complete purchase. A .com is about US$10.50 a year, with whois privacy included. The contact details must be your real name and address (domain rules require it); whois privacy keeps them out of public view.

Why not sign in to Cloudflare as hello@etudechess.com: that address only works while the domain and its email routing work, and both live in this Cloudflare account. If the domain ever lapsed or routing broke, the password-reset email for the account that fixes it would have nowhere to go. Keeping the Cloudflare login on your Gmail avoids that loop. Everything else can use hello@.

2 · The email address you

  1. Cloudflare dashboard: Compute → Email Service → Email Routing → Onboard Domain, choose etudechess.com, Done. Cloudflare adds the mail records itself; allow 5 to 15 minutes.
  2. Email Routing → Destination Addresses: add your personal Gmail address, then open the email Cloudflare sends there and press Verify email address.
  3. Back in Email Routing: select etudechess.com → Routing Rules → Create routing rule. Email pattern hello, action Send to an email, destination your Gmail. Save.
  4. Test it: from a different email account than your Gmail (Gmail can hide mail it thinks it sent to itself), send something to hello@etudechess.com. It should arrive in your Gmail; check spam if not.
  5. In Gmail, make a filter for mail to hello@etudechess.com that adds a label Étude, so it's easy to find.

3 · The Google account you

  1. In a private window, go to accounts.google.com/signup. Name: Étude and Chess (it's what people see, and can be changed later).
  2. When it offers a Gmail address, choose Use your existing email instead and enter hello@etudechess.com. Google sends a code there; it arrives in your Gmail; enter it.
  3. Recovery phone: yours. Recovery email: your personal Gmail. Turn on 2-step verification. Password in your password manager.

This is a Google login with no Gmail inbox: mail to hello@ still comes through Cloudflare to your Gmail. Use it for “Sign in with Google” on the social platforms and any tools Étude needs, never for anything personal.

4 · The GitHub organisation and repo you

  1. On github.com, signed in as yourself: your picture → Your organizations → New organization → the Free plan.
  2. Name etudechess. Contact email: hello@etudechess.com. “This organization belongs to: My personal account.”
  3. In the organisation: New repository, name etude, Private or Public (both work), no README. Create repository.
  4. On your PC, in a terminal in Documents: git clone https://github.com/etudechess/etude.git
  5. In the Claude desktop app, connect the new Documents\etude folder to this chat, and tell me.

GitHub allows one free personal account per person, which is why this is an organisation under your own login rather than a second account.

5 · Claim the name on the social platforms you

Register etudechess everywhere you might use it, even ones you won't use yet, so the name is the same everywhere and nobody else takes it. Use Sign in with Google with the Étude Google account where it's offered; otherwise sign up with hello@etudechess.com. Keep every password in your password manager, in one Étude folder.

  • Instagram (and Threads, which comes with it): a professional account, category “App page” or “Education”.
  • TikTok, YouTube (a channel on the Étude Google account, handle @etudechess), X, Bluesky.
  • Facebook: a Page (Pages hang off a personal profile; that's normal and isn't shown publicly).
  • Reddit: the user u/etudechess; post as yourself in chess communities rather than as the brand, as their rules usually require.
  • Discord: a server, if you want a place for players.
  • Lichess and chess.com: an etudechess account on each, for posting puzzles and articles where chess players already are.

Bio for each, the same everywhere: “Étude Chess: chess practice that explains itself. Free, in your browser. etudechess.com”. If Hana appears on the account, add that she's Étude's AI-generated character (see “Promoting it” below).

Some platforms ask for a phone number: yours is fine. Turn on two-factor authentication on each.

6 · Fill the repo me, then you

I put in:

  • site/: the app, talking to the server at its own address (etudechess.com/u/…).
  • server/worker.js: the server, accepting only etudechess.com and www.etudechess.com.
  • wrangler.toml and package.json at the top: a Worker named etude that serves site/ and runs the server for everything else, with its own fresh Durable Object setup and your account id as admin.
  • A commit.bat for the new folder, like your current one.

Then you run that commit.bat.

7 · Create the Worker from the repo you

  1. In the new Cloudflare account: Workers & Pages → Create → Import a repository (it may say Connect to Git).
  2. Connect GitHub when asked. On GitHub's screen, install Cloudflare's app on the etudechess organisation, for the etude repository only.
  3. Pick etudechess/etude. Settings: name etude (it must match wrangler.toml), Build command empty, Deploy command npx wrangler deploy, Root directory empty.
  4. Create and deploy. If asked for a workers.dev subdomain, pick anything short.
  5. When the build finishes, open the …workers.dev address Cloudflare shows: it should be Étude itself.

If the build fails, open it, copy the last 20 or so lines of its log, and paste them to me.

8 · Put it on the domain you

  1. Workers & Pages → etude → Settings → Domains & Routes → Add → Custom domain: etudechess.com. Cloudflare adds the DNS record and certificate itself; give it a few minutes.
  2. The same again for www.etudechess.com.
  3. Open https://etudechess.com and try it with a test account: start a game against it from a second device, play a few moves and a chat message, then delete the test account. Don't make your real account here: it comes across in step 10.

9 · Turn the old address into a redirect me, then you

Before this step, finish or resign any online games you care about: they stay on the old server.

I replace chess/index.html in your website repo with a small page that, when opened:

  • reads your account key, name, settings and puzzle rating from the old address;
  • turns off notifications for the old address;
  • opens https://etudechess.com with those in the link, where the new site saves them and clears them from the address bar.

Then you push the website repo with your usual commit.bat.

10 · Move each device you, and each friend

  1. Open the old address (or the old home-screen icon) once. It lands on etudechess.com, signed in as you, with your name and code as before.
  2. Your account → Share friend link, and send it to friends who have moved; or open theirs.
  3. Your account → Notifications: turn them on again.
  4. Add etudechess.com to the home screen and delete the old icon. On iPhone this is needed for notifications.

Anyone who never opens the old link can still come across later with their key (Your account → Show key → Copy link, opened on the new site), as long as the redirect page stays up.

11 · Admin nothing to do

Your account keeps its id, which goes into the new wrangler.toml in step 6, so Your account → Admin → Accounts works on the new site from the start.

12 · Retire the old server you, later

Once everyone has moved, which you can see in the old app's Accounts screen (last active dates), delete the old Worker: in your current Cloudflare account, Workers & Pages → etude-tables → Settings → Delete. Leave the redirect page in your website repo for good, so old links keep working.

Promoting it, with Hana as Étude's character

Hana can be the face of Étude as an openly AI-generated character: a mascot and presenter, not a person.

Where she fits

  • The site: a short “Meet Hana, Étude's AI-generated mascot” section on the home or About page. Not on the app's working screens, which stay plain.
  • Étude's social accounts (step 5): “Hana's puzzle of the week”, a tactic in 30 seconds, launch and feature announcements. You write the chess; she presents it.
  • A learning story told as fiction: “Follow Hana learning the London with Étude's opening guide.”
  • Optionally in the app later: an illustrated version of her as the coach's avatar, or on a cosmetic unlock.

Rules that keep it honest

  • Say she's AI everywhere she appears: in each account's bio (for example “Étude's AI-generated character”), on the site, and with each platform's AI label when posting (Instagram, TikTok and YouTube all have one for realistic AI images).
  • No testimonials or results from her (“I went from 800 to 1200 with Étude”). A made-up person vouching for a product is misleading under Australian Consumer Law. Showing how a feature works is fine.
  • She never poses as a user: no reviews, comments or community posts in her name pretending to be a player.
  • She isn't the founder or the team. About page: made by you; Hana is the character.

Before using her

  • Keep her Étude content chess-only, and decide whether to link it to her other account at all: Étude's audience includes children and people you know professionally. If that other content doesn't fit, keep the two unlinked, or give Étude a separate character.
  • Check the licence of the model she was trained on allows commercial use of its images, and that she doesn't closely resemble a real person.
  • Protect the name and logo, not the pictures: in many places AI-generated images can't be copyrighted, so others could reuse them.

Later

  • Sending as hello@etudechess.com: Cloudflare only forwards incoming mail. To reply from hello@, add it in Gmail under Settings → Accounts → “Send mail as”, which needs an outgoing mail service (several have free tiers for small volumes). Worth doing once people write in.
  • Donations: a Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee or GitHub Sponsors link (the organisation can receive Sponsors). Before taking money, drop the piece sets licensed CC BY-NC-SA (non-commercial), or get permission from their artists.
  • A privacy page: what the server keeps (a name, friends, games, chat, notification addresses), where (Cloudflare), and how to delete it (Delete account).
  • Credits: Stockfish is GPL. Keep a credits page that links to its source, and to the app's repo if it's public.
  • Google Play, if wanted: the web app can be packaged as an Android app (a Trusted Web Activity). Google charges US$25 once, and a new personal developer account has to run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days before it can publish.

Going back

Until step 12, nothing old is removed: restore chess/index.html from its backup in Claude outputs and push, and the old address and old server work as they do now. Accounts made or moved on the new server in the meantime stay there.
